# After the Federal Helium Reserve: A Multi-Method Framework for Critical-Input Security

A long-form policy report (CSIS/Brookings-caliber, UVA-published) composing four analytical methods (STDN-GEN, lia/MEKH, multiplex trade-network analysis, MRIO/GTAP) into a single workflow for critical-input security, with Qatar helium as the worked case and Qatar LNG + microelectronic precursors as validation cases. Hook: the 2024 wind-down of the Federal Helium Reserve.

## 1. Paper identity

**Working title:** *After the Federal Helium Reserve: A Multi-Method Framework for Critical-Input Security*

**Alternate titles considered:**
- *The Qatar Stress Test: Helium, LNG, and a Framework for US Critical-Input Security*
- *Visible Networks, Invisible Risks: Policy Analysis for the Post-Reserve Era*
- *From Dependency Maps to Cascading Impact: A Policy Analytical Stack for Critical Inputs*

**Thesis:**
The 2024 wind-down of the Federal Helium Reserve eliminated US stockpile insurance for a process consumable that at least 152 tracked technologies across microelectronics, biotechnology, and pharmaceuticals depend on — just as Qatar-linked geopolitical shocks have demonstrated the real economic cost of concentrated upstream supply. Existing vulnerability-analysis methods are fragmented. This report composes four of them — bottom-up material-transformation mapping (MEKH / lia), top-down technology dependency networks (STDN-GEN), multiplex trade-network analysis (Comtrade-based lock-in and coalition modeling), and economy-wide cascading-impact modeling (MRIO / GTAP) — into a single analytical stack. Applied to the Qatar helium scenario as a worked case and validated against Qatar LNG (same country, different substitution profile) and microelectronic precursors (different input type, same framework), the stack supports four near-term policy responses: a right-sized Strategic Helium Reserve, a Critical Industrial Gas designation, helium-recycling mandates at federally-funded end users, and Canadian infrastructure backstop financing under DPA Title III.

**Target reader:** CSIS/Brookings-audience policy professional — not a specialist, but technically literate. Congressional staff, agency program leads, commissioned analysts.

**Length target:** 50–70 pages including figures, ~20k–28k words of prose, in CSIS long-form report format. Executive summary + body + appendix.

## 4. The four-layer analytical stack

The central contribution is the composition of four existing methods into one policy analytical workflow. Each is already documented in its own paper; this report positions them as a coherent stack.

1. **Bottom-up material-transformation mapping** — lia / MEKH (AAMAS 2025). Directed hypergraphs over ore → intermediate → refined-input transformations. Captures hidden intermediates and process-consumable dependencies invisible to top-down analyses.
2. **Top-down technology dependency networks** — STDN-GEN (SIGIR 2026). Multi-agent debate over a 4-layer DAG (technology → component → material → country). Provides horizontal coverage across many technologies with evidence-scored, agent-generated dependencies and a 650-element controlled ontology for normalization.
3. **Multiplex trade-network analysis** — Vullikanti et al. microelectronic precursors (in draft). Comtrade HS-6 trade data organized as multiplex networks; quantifies lock-in, substitutability, leverage ratios, and coalition structure.
4. **Economy-wide cascading-impact modeling** — Mishra et al. Qatar gas (PNAS Nexus draft). MRIO + linear programming over GTAP 12 (163 countries, 65 sectors). Supports disruption, reallocation, and capacity-expansion scenarios.

Target word budgets are targets, not ceilings.

1. **Executive Summary with vignette (~1,500 w).** March 2026 Ras Laffan strikes and a counterfactual question: what if the same geopolitics hit a Qatari input with *no* substitution channel? The FHR is gone. Here's what the stack reveals and the four policy responses it supports.
2. **Introduction: The Policy Vacuum (~2,000 w).** FHR history and 2024 wind-down. Helium as a process consumable that existing critical-minerals frameworks miss. Why Qatar matters structurally (LNG + helium co-location at Ras Laffan, Strait of Hormuz). The analytical gap: no single existing method captures both technology-level exposure and economy-wide cascading impact.
3. **The Four-Layer Analytical Stack (~4,000 w).** One subsection per layer, ~800–1,000 words: what it does, what it uniquely reveals, what it cannot answer alone, cited methods paper.
4. **Helium: The Worked Case (~5,500 w).** Walks helium through all four layers. Scenario: 40% Qatar cut.
   - 4.1 MEKH: helium as process consumable, not constituent
   - 4.2 STDN-GEN: 152 technologies across 3 domains; HHI 2,815; Qatar 38.8% / US 35.3%
   - 4.3 Trade-network: Canada pivot 2022–2025; lock-in metric; coalition options
   - 4.4 GTAP extension: estimated economy-wide impact, scaled from Qatar gas scenarios with substitutability parameters adjusted
5. **Validation Case A: Qatar LNG (~2,500 w).** Summary of Mishra et al. findings; demonstrates MRIO layer works at scale on a well-understood commodity. Cross-reference to what helium lacks (substitutability, reallocation capacity).
6. **Validation Case B: Microelectronic Precursors (~2,500 w).** Summary of Vullikanti et al. findings; demonstrates multiplex/lock-in framework generalizes across input types. Cross-reference to helium's lock-in profile.
7. **Policy Recommendations (~4,000 w).** Two tiers: framework-level (what the stack enables for policy process) and helium-specific (four concrete actions). Tier 3 items flagged honestly as "where further analysis would strengthen."
8. **Limitations and Future Work (~1,000 w).** Demo-dataset caveats, agent-estimated fallbacks, MRIO comparative-statics boundary, scope limits of multi-method stacks.
9. **Appendix (~1,500 w).** Method details per layer; data sources; reproducibility pointers (GitHub, paper citations); glossary of terms (STDN, MEKH, HHI, MRIO, GTAP, DPA, FHR).

## 6. Policy recommendations (locked)

**Tier 1 — Analytical capacity-building. All included.**
- Establish a standing multi-tier critical-input dependency analysis capability at DOE, Commerce, or USGS. Productionize the stack; fund it.
- Require agencies to commission multi-method risk assessments before major critical-technology procurements (CHIPS, IRA, DPA programs).
- Expand USGS *Mineral Commodity Summaries* to cover industrial gases and process consumables.

**Tier 2 — Specific policy mechanisms. All four included.**
- Re-establish a Strategic Helium Reserve, right-sized from the stack's disruption modeling (e.g., X months of US demand for high-HHI, low-substitutability process consumables).
- Add helium (and neon, xenon, argon) to the USGS Critical Minerals List, or create a parallel Critical Industrial Gases designation. Unlocks DPA authorities, tax credits, reporting requirements.
- Mandate helium recycling at federally-funded MRI and semiconductor facilities (DOE/CHIPS fabs, NIH imaging).
- DPA Title III backstop financing for Canadian helium infrastructure (pipeline, purification, storage). Rationale: 97% of current US imports are Canadian; modest infrastructure co-investment is cheap insurance relative to modeled cascading-impact losses.

**Tier 3 — Flagged as "where further analysis would strengthen":**
- Specific dollar figures and legislative vehicles (NDAA 2027 amendments, DPA funding levels, reserve capitalization).
- DOE Critical Materials Innovation Hub program for process-gas substitution R&D (nitrogen/argon for leak-testing where feasible).
- Strategic helium purchasing agreement with Algeria as non-OECD, non-Russia diversifier.
- Bilateral US–Canada Critical Input Compact framework, helium as pilot commodity.

## 7. Key empirical anchors (from stdn-explorer + video + companion papers)

- 152 technologies depend on helium across 3 domains in the Explorer dataset
- Qatar 38.8% / US 35.3% / Algeria 5.9% of global helium production (HHI 2,815)
- 40% Qatar cut → 15.5% global supply reduction
- US direct imports shifted from Qatar (through 2021) to Canada (2022–2025, ~97%). Price-shock risk and import-exposure risk are now different questions with different answers.
- Only 2 countries have held the top US supplier position in 9 years → severe lock-in
- March 2026 Iranian strikes on Ras Laffan / Mesaieed removed ~17% of Qatar's LNG export capacity (cited in GTAP paper)
- Co-product helium export reduction from Ras Laffan gas disruption: ~14% (GTAP paper Discussion)
- Taiwan and South Korea import ~69% and ~64.7% of helium from Qatar respectively

## 8. Data and methodological caveats to surface honestly

- Explorer demo dataset is 180 technologies × 3 domains; USGS-grounded country data where available, agent-estimated fallbacks otherwise.
- GTAP Qatar paper and microelectronic precursors paper are both draft-stage; citation should be as working papers, noting the distinction.
- MRIO is comparative-statics, not transient dynamics. LP-optimal reallocation is an upper bound on feasible mitigation.
- GTAP 2023 baseline vs. 2026 events: global LNG trade restructured between these years; results should be read directionally.
- Tier 3 recommendations require modeling work outside current tool output.
